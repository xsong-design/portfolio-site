> Also, I'm currently looking at the driver tip project. So, I don't know where some of the context and problems come from, but originally this project started because of COVID. So, COVID during the lockdown, all these items like eggs and tissue papers got super expensive, and people had to stay at home and they couldn't go to stores. So, that's when Walmart was still in partnership with DoorDash to deliver their groceries. However, as you can imagine, DoorDash is DoorDash has an inconsistent workforce. Sometimes these delivery drivers switch between DoorDash, Uber, and whatever other services based on the amount of pay they get. So sometimes it's very inconsistent. And for groceries, we usually have something called time slots. So a user has to select a time slot of when their groceries get delivered before they even start shopping because groceries are time sensitive. So Based on how many drivers we have at the time, we potentially have different set amounts of time slots that people can reserve. As you can imagine, after work, like five to seven was the most busy, and it was very hard for us to give our customers time slots that suited their needs. So at this point, Walmart wanted to build out their own driver fleet instead of relying only on DoorDash. And by doing so, Walmart needed to. Provide not only a competitive salary, but also a competitive driver tipping experience where drivers are more incentivized to stay with Walmart rather than jump between apps. That was Walmart's goal to build and retain a workforce.

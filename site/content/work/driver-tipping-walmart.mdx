This work came about because of COVID. People could not go to stores, so Walmart delivered groceries through DoorDash. Those drivers jump between apps depending on pay. Grocery time slots are time-sensitive, and we could not meet demand. Walmart wanted its own Spark fleet. More drivers meant more slots. Tipping had to help keep that fleet.

## After delivery, the tip disappeared

With DoorDash, the tip sat after delivery. Once groceries arrived, most people did not come back. Those tips were lost.

I designed moving the ask to checkout — while someone is already paying. We looked at competitors first. Two things had to be obvious there: 100% of the tip goes to the driver, and it only applies to grocery delivery.

## Grocery only, in a mixed cart

Store groceries and online fulfillment were merging into one app. A cart could mix items a driver brings from the store with items that ship. Walmart customers are budget-sensitive. They needed to feel they could tip, and to see that the tip was for the grocery delivery, not the whole mixed cart.

My job was that checkout moment: when to ask, how to say the money goes to the driver, and how to keep it on groceries only.

## What we tried

We tried percent of the order (15%, 20%) against set dollars ($3, $5). We tried an empty tipping module against a preselected amount — on unless the customer removes it. We tried putting the module on its own, right above the wallet, versus inside the grocery cart, where it reads as part of that order.

Those three questions are what the final had to answer.

## Takeaways

COVID showed up while I was on Search & Browse. I moved to checkout — fulfillment, the bottom of the funnel — mainly to take on more responsibility. Search and browse is nice to have. The real complexity sits in checkout. Business goals changed with COVID. I had to get adaptive: sit with business partners to name the problem, then solve it.

What I would keep is that communication. Not only UX, engineering, and PM. At the bottom of the funnel you also need business and legal in the room, and a real working relationship with both.

What that taught me as I grew into a senior designer: the job is not only the customer, or the business of that customer. It is every party in the mix, and one solution that still holds.
